TakeMeter: Does fine tuning beat a prompted LLM?

2026 | Individual project

A text classifier that sorts TV-discussion posts into analysis, reaction, or prediction, built to measure discourse quality in an online community.

PythonHugging FaceDistilBERTGroq Llama 3.3 70BGoogle Colabscikit-learn metricsGradio

Problem

Can a small, fine tuned model match a large prompted LLM on a nuanced labeling task? And if not, why?

What I did

  • Collected and hand-labeled 215 Reddit posts using a written taxonomy and explicit edge-case decision rules (balanced: 36% / 33% / 31%).
  • Built a 70/15/15 stratified split with a locked 33-example test set.
  • Fine-tuned DistilBERT on a Colab T4 GPU and compared it with a zero-shot Llama 3.3 70B baseline.
  • Reported per-class precision, recall, and F1, a confusion matrix, confidence calibration, and an analysis of every error.

Provenance Guard: Explaining an AI detection decision

2026 | Individual project

A backend API for creative-writing platforms that estimates whether text is human- or AI-written, explains the result in plain language, logs every decision, and lets creators appeal.

PythonFlaskGroq LLMStylometricsREST APIAudit loggingRate limiting

Design

  • Two independent signals: an LLM judgment (60%) and three stylometric statistics computed in pure Python: sentence length variance, type/token ratio, and punctuation diversity (40%).
  • Asymmetric thresholds: it takes more evidence to call text AI-generated than human-written, because a false accusation is the costlier error on a creative platform.
  • Every decision is written to an audit trail; an appeals endpoint flags disputed results for human review.
  • Per-IP rate limiting (10 per minute, 100 per day) to prevent abuse.

Known limitation I documented

Careful, formal writing by non-native English speakers can look like AI on both signals. That is a real fairness risk that would need an additional signal to address.
